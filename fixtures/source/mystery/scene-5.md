They gathered in the study at dusk, the lamps lit against the early dark. Mrs. Brandt, the cook, drew the curtains while the constable took the door.

Julian drew out the gold watch and wound it, unhurried, as if time kept for him alone.

Mara laid the new will on the desk between them, still folded. "Not a shilling for the nephew," Mrs. Brandt murmured from the window, before Mara had said a word of what the vault held.

"You knew about the floorboard," Mara said to Julian. "You knew before I told anyone, because you watched him hide the key the night he died. The night you were not in the city."

Julian's hand stopped on the watch stem. The rain had stopped too, and the room was very quiet.
