Julian Vane kept rooms above the inn, and it was there Mara found him, unpacked and unhurried. Rain ticked against the inn's dark glass while he poured himself a drink he did not offer to share.

"I was in the city all week," he said. "Ask anyone on the night coach."

His questions came carelessly, and then one arrived that was not careless at all. "The key you pried from under the floorboard," he said. "Did it open anything worth my uncle dying for?"

Mara had told no one about the floorboard. She let the silence hold until it had weight, and Julian filled it the way nervous men do, with talk of his uncle's habits and his own long absence. He smiled like a man rehearsing grief.
