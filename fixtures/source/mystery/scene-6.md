The constable took Julian down the front steps in the grey of morning, and Julian confessed before they reached the gate, in the flat voice of a man explaining arithmetic: the new will had left him nothing, and he had known it a month.

Mrs. Brandt saw the house closed room by room. Rain ticked against the glass as Mara finished her notes at Edmund's desk, in the study where it had begun.

There would be papers, and a trial, and the slow settling of the estate into other hands. The house kept its silence, and the case of Edmund Vane was closed.
