Rain ticked against the glass of the study's tall window when Mara Voss arrived. Edmund Vane lay where he had fallen, sprawled beneath the portrait of his father, and Dr. Hale's first glance only confirmed what the household already knew: Edmund Vane was dead.

Mrs. Brandt, the housekeeper, stood at the door with her hands knotted in her apron. She had kept the house for thirty years, she said, and never once locked the study.

Mara noted what was absent. The brass key Edmund wore on his watch chain was gone, though the chain itself remained. The gold watch was another matter entirely; that had been lost in the river years ago, along with much else of the old estate. The hound Bruno had died last winter, and his basket still sat cold by the hearth.

One instruction survived its author, recorded in Edmund's own hand and witnessed by his solicitor: the sealed letter was to be read aloud at the will reading, and not before.
